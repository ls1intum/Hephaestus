Use your Practice profile to read feedback, choose a next step, and check the evidence behind your practice standing.
Other developers cannot see it.
They see your results only as part of the counts on [Practices across the workspace](#practices-across-the-workspace).
[Your data in Hephaestus](./privacy.mdx#ai-processing-and-visibility) lists who else can.

The profile uses practice review results about your work on pull requests, merge requests, issues, conversations, and documents.
This includes the reviews you write.
Feedback on a pull request, merge request or issue addresses its author’s work.
Feedback about your reviewing stays on your own Practice profile or in your own conversation.
It never compares you with other developers.

Results from an earlier version of practice review stay in your history, with a note that their evidence cannot be verified.
Earlier feedback cards are marked as unverified history.
They do not show whether the current standard is met and do not count as current proof.
You can still read their retained text and evidence when access to their sources is available.

## Opening your Practice profile

1. Select a workspace.
2. Under **Dashboards**, select **Practice profile**.

A workspace that reviews practices also opens here by default.
A workspace that does not review practices has no Practice profile and opens on [Activity](./activity.mdx).

## Reading the top of the page

The header explains what Hephaestus reviews and how feedback resolves.
The chip beside the title reads **Latest review** with the date and reviewed work.
It can instead say that the review stopped before it finished.
While a review runs, the chip reads **Review running** and names the work.
Both states open [Reviews of your work](#reviews-of-your-work).

A review appears here after it records a result about your work.
The chip keeps your latest recorded review even when it is older than the standing’s 90-day window or its evidence is hidden.
Before your first recorded review, the chip reads **No review yet**.

A ring counts your practices by standing:

- **Going well**
- **Mixed feedback**
- **Needs attention**
- **Nothing to report yet**
- **Not reviewed**

**See all practice groups** opens the table of practice groups.
**Going well** follows the header. It shows practices your work keeps meeting, with the work that supports each statement.
[Your feedback](#your-feedback) follows **Going well**.

A practice that your workspace no longer reviews leaves the page, with its standing and its feedback.

## Your feedback

1. Select **Open** to see feedback that still needs a response or more reviewed work.
2. Read the evidence and next step on a card.
3. Decide what to change, or record your response below the card.

For example, feedback about an unclear purpose can help you improve the description of your next pull request.

Each card describes one repeated pattern in your work.
It states what reviews observed, on which work, and when.
It also gives one next step to try.
Feedback posted on a piece of work stays there and is not a card.
[Feedback on a piece of work](#feedback-on-a-piece-of-work) tells you how to find it.
The tabs show cards newest first:

- **Open** shows each open card. The page opens on this tab.
- **Resolved and closed** shows each resolved or closed card.
- **All** shows each card, including withdrawn cards.

A card resolves in one of two ways:

- **By your work.** Three consecutive pieces of reviewed work with no problem on the practice resolve the card. It reads *Resolved by the work* and names that work. Work that shows the problem again resets the count. **Work reviewed for this practice** on the card shows the count.
- **By you.** Your response can mark the card *Marked as addressed* or *Marked as not applicable*, with the date. The card then shows **Marked by you** without the green color of a resolution, because your next work confirms it. The count of clean work continues.

To record or undo your response:

1. Under **Or mark it yourself**, select **I fixed this** or **Does not fit my work**.
   Point to or focus an answer to read what it does.
2. To undo your response, select it again.

Both answers move the card to **Marked by you**.
After **Does not fit my work**, a later review of the same work does not send you that point again.
The card opens again when you undo the response.

If you think that the feedback is wrong, select **Disagree** beside **Not helpful**.
Write one or two sentences on what is wrong, then select **Send**.
Workspace admins read your sentence and can correct the results or withdraw the card.
The card stays open until then.
**Disagree** saves a disagreement on the feedback.
To take the disagreement back, select **Disagree** again.

If your workspace changes a practice after a review wrote its card, the card closes without resolution.
It reads *Closed*, gives the date and reason, and stops counting work that shows no problem on the practice.
A closed card stays on the page for 30 days after closure.
A newer card for the same practice replaces the open card, so each practice has at most one open card.

A workspace admin can withdraw a card with incorrect words.
If you already saw the card, a recent withdrawal appears under **All** as a short notice, without the card’s text.
A card you had not seen never appears.
A withdrawn card accepts no new response.
It resolved nothing and says nothing about your standing.
The results stay unchanged.

Later reviews and Heph do not receive the withdrawn text.
If the admin restores the card, it returns as it was.

To rate a card:

1. At the bottom right of the card, select **Helpful** or **Not helpful**.
   Each is a small button with an icon. Point to it to read its name.
2. Add a note about why. The note is optional.

A rating says whether the feedback helped you.
It does not record a disagreement with the card.
While a disagreement stands, its sentence is the one note on the card, so a rating adds no note.
If you take back your rating, the disagreement stays.

Workspace admins can see that you have a current answer to feedback and when that record was made.
A rating without an answer, including its time, stays private.
Workspace admins read the sentence of a current disagreement on the results behind it.
They can mark a result incorrect or withdraw the card.
The card text stays private to you.
Nobody else sees your other notes.

### Reading a card

Each card has four parts:

1. The top part says what the feedback is about.
   Its explanation is under **Why this was raised**.
2. **Next step** says what to do.
3. **Work reviewed for this practice** says how the feedback resolves.
   For example, it resolves after three pieces of reviewed work come back clean.
   It shows your reviewed work along a line, oldest to newest, with the date of each piece under it.
   On the right are the count of clean work and your answers.
   On a narrow screen they are under the line.
4. The bottom part gives the date and your rating.

The line shows up to five of the most recent pieces of work behind this feedback, ordered by when they were reviewed.
One mark at its start counts the earlier pieces that are not shown.
Each piece has the same icon as its results.
A wrench shows work that needed attention, and a check in a circle shows clean work.

A thin upright mark with the caption **Feedback created** separates the work before the feedback from the work after it.
It appears only when the line includes work reviewed before the feedback was created.
Clean work before the feedback has a grey check, because only clean work after the feedback counts.
The clean pieces in a row after the feedback are joined in green.
Small empty dots show how many more clean pieces resolve the feedback.

To open the practice, select anywhere on the card or **Learn more about this practice**.
The links, the buttons, and the work on the line keep their own action.

## Reviews of your work

The title chip opens the reviews that recorded a result about your work, newest first and grouped by day.
A review that still runs may not appear yet, because a review appears only after it records a result about your work.
The newest review has the **Latest** tag.

A review that someone requested has the **Requested** tag.
It is a real review and retains earlier results in history.
The newest eligible result follows recorded order, regardless of who started it.
An older review cannot replace newer proof.
A marked current result does not make an earlier result current again.
Repository scans have no developer and do not move your personal standing.

Each row names the work and links to the original.
It starts with the practices to improve: the first two by name, then a count of the rest.
If there are none, it says *Nothing to improve*, or it says that the review stopped early or still runs.
Below that, it counts each practice once by its outcome: **Met**, **Does not apply**, or **Could not determine**.
It then counts the practices the review reached, including those with no recorded result about you.

For feedback on GitLab, **Read the feedback on the merge request** opens the comment.
Elsewhere, the row counts the pieces of feedback that reached you.
**Show** filters the list by kind of work.
**Timeframe** filters it to the last 7, 30, or 90 days.
**All time** is the initial value.
With either filter on, no row shows the **Latest** tag, because the newest included review might not be your latest.

Older reviews load when you scroll to the end of the list.
You can also select **View earlier reviews**.
If older reviews cannot load, select **Retry**.
After the whole list loads without filters, later reviews of the same work show their sequence, such as *2nd review*.
**Request review** appears beside a row when you may request another review as author, assignee, or workspace admin.

**Open review** opens a review.
It shows the link to the original work, the date, and the number of practices reached.
It also shows **Request review** when you may request a review.
Two tabs follow:

- **Every practice** lists the practices behind the review. Each shows its result or explains why it sent no feedback. During a review, practices appear as the review reaches them.
- **What we noticed** lists all recorded events about the work in order. It shows what happened, how the review noticed it, and whether a review started.

In **Every practice**, these filters apply:

- **Group** selects one practice group.
- **Review on** selects an event, such as new commits pushed.
- **Practice** filters names by the text you enter.

## The practice group table

**See all practice groups** opens the **All practice groups** panel.
Each row shows a workspace practice group, its standing, and a ring that splits its practices by standing.
A sentence describes what changed since the previous review.
New feedback has no sentence here, because its card says it.
The rows appear by standing, with groups that need attention first.

1. To reverse the order, select the **Standing** heading.

Some practices and groups have nothing to report yet.
They fold into lines at the end of their table, so that they do not read as a gap:

- **Does not apply to your work yet**: every review in the last 90 days found that they do not apply.
- **Practices not reviewed** and **Practice groups not reviewed**: no review in the last 90 days has reviewed them.
  A group also folds here when none of its practices applies or was reviewed.
  A group with no practice to review also folds here.
  That is a group with no practices, or with all of them switched off.

To see the rows of a fold, select its line.
The rows open in the table above it.
Select a row to open the practice or the group.
A practice or group that does not apply to your work yet says so in its row and in its own panel.

## Opening a group and a practice

1. Select a group row.
2. To see more detail, select a practice.

The group panel describes the group and lists each practice with its standing and trend, and what changed.
The practice panel starts with an introduction:

- Why the practice matters.
- A picture of the idea, if the practice has one. A screen reader reads its description.
- What work that meets the practice looks like. If the practice has a picture, this text is its caption.

Tabs follow the introduction:

- **Feedback** shows the feedback about this practice. The panel opens on it.
- **Results** shows results by review, with response controls.
- **Guide** explains the practice in more depth. It can show more pictures and links. It appears only for a practice that has a guide.

To open a practice from a feedback card, select **Learn more about this practice**.

A practice with open feedback shows the label **Has open feedback** beside its name.
The label also shows the clean work since then, as on the card.
It is a label, not a button.

### Feedback on a piece of work

Feedback posted on a pull request, merge request, or issue stays on that work, not under **Feedback**.

1. Open the practice panel.
2. Select the **Results** tab.
3. Open the result.

Under **Feedback**, select **Read the feedback comment** to open a recorded comment.
If several comments carry the feedback, their links appear together.
If no comment link was recorded, use the result’s link to the original work.

A standing is read from your newest reviewed work, up to four pieces for each practice.
Your latest work counts most, and older work counts less.

To see how much work a standing rests on, point at its badge, move to it with the keyboard, or tap it.
The trend beside the badge opens its own sentence in the same way.
A tap opens the sentence, not the row.
To close the sentence, press Escape or select a different part of the page.

The sentence names the work it is read from.
It also tells you how much of your work was reviewed for the practice or the group in the last 90 days.
For example: "Read from your last 4 reviewed pull requests. In the last 90 days, 11 pull requests were reviewed for this practice."

A piece of work counts when a review could judge the practice on it.
Eligible decided results provide the evidence for these counts.
A request is not a separate evidence-only class.
A group counts each piece of work once, even when several of its practices judged it.

If fewer than three pieces of work back a standing, its sentence calls it an early read.
The next piece of work can change an early read.

Each panel opens over the previous panel, with the page visible behind it.
The short path above the title shows where you are, from the page to the panel.

1. To go to a position on the path, select it.
2. To go back one level, select **Back**.

### Switching to the workspace

The foot of **All practice groups**, of a group, and of a practice has a button to the same place on [Practices across the workspace](#practices-across-the-workspace).
On a practice, the workspace page marks that practice in its group.
**See your own standing** on the workspace page goes back to your profile.
However often you switch, one **Back** returns to where you started.
An instance admin who reads your profile with **View as user** does not see the button.

## Practices across the workspace

Practices across the workspace shows how each practice goes across your workspace.
It counts developers and results and never names a developer.
Each result comes from AI review of the work and can be wrong, so it is not a grade.
Your own results stay in your Practice profile.
The page keeps its explanations behind an info button (i).
Point at the button, move to it with the keyboard, or tap it to read the explanation.

1. In the sidebar, under **Practice profile**, select **Across the workspace**.
   If the sidebar shows only icons, select the **Across the workspace** icon.

### Results by practice group

**All practice groups** has one row for each practice group.
The line under the title tells how many developers the workspace reviews.
Each row shows how many practices the group has and how many results were **Met**, **Not met**, **Does not apply**, and **Could not determine**.
A row with no results shows **No results yet**.

### Small counts

The page shows every count, however small, and everyone sees the same counts.
It never names a developer, but a small count can tell other members about your results:

- If few developers have results for a practice, a member who knows them can tell how your work went.
- A member who compares the page before and after your next review can see the counts change.

A workspace admin can hide you from the workspace. Then the page does not count you.

### Open a group

1. Select **Open group**.
   A panel shows the group's practices, with **Developers with results** and the result counts for each practice.
2. To see your own results, select **See your own standing** at the foot of the panel.
   To go to one practice in your profile, select **Open in your Practice profile** on its row.

At the end of the page, **See your own standing** opens **All practice groups** in your profile.

## When nothing has been reviewed yet

Before the first review, each practice counts as **Not reviewed**.
The chip reads **No review yet** and no feedback appears.
The first review with a recorded result fills the page.
To request a review of eligible work, use **Request review** on [Activity](./activity.mdx#request-a-review).
