## How to do it

- Start with your goal. Say what you are trying to do.
- Say what you already tried and what happened after each try.
- Say exactly what happened, and what you expected. For example, instead of “Why does the build fail?”, write “I’m trying to run the tests on my laptop. After a clean install, `npm test` stops with ‘Cannot find module config’. Reinstalling did not help.”
- Before you send, read your question as a teammate who does not know your work.
- Add what they would have to ask first, unless earlier messages already give it.

## When it does not apply

- Your message is not a question. For example, it is a statement, a thank-you or social chat.
- The conversation has not settled yet, so there is no finished thread to read.

It still applies when your question leans on earlier messages in the same thread. Those messages count, so you do not need to repeat them.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that every question needs a long background. When you and your teammate clearly share the context, a quick question is fine. Add only what your teammate would otherwise have to ask for.
- Writing only “Help, it’s broken”. Your teammate can only guess what you did. Instead, say what you ran, what you saw and what you expected.
- Sharing an error with no explanation. An error alone does not say what you ran or what you expected. Instead, describe what you ran, what happened and what you expected.
- Pasting your whole program or the whole log. Instead, share just enough code and output for a teammate to see the problem.

## Sources

- [How do I ask a good question?](https://stackoverflow.com/help/how-to-ask), Stack Overflow Help Center. Explain the problem, copy errors as text, and check that someone without your context can understand the question.
- [How to report bugs effectively](https://www.chiark.greenend.org.uk/~sgtatham/bugs.html), Simon Tatham, 1999. Report what you did, what you saw and what you expected to see, so the programmer can help.
- [An empirical study of question discussions on Stack Overflow](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-022-10180-z), Zhu, Zhang, Hassan and Godfrey, 2022. Questions with more comments were answered more slowly, but the study shows a link, not a cause.
