## How to do it

- Start with where the work stands, such as done, in progress or blocked.
- If you are stuck, name the blocker plainly. For example, instead of “Still stuck”, write “Blocked: the test server fails because its access key is missing”.
- Say what would clear the blocker, such as access, a decision or a review. Naming someone who can help can make the request clearer.
- If teammates need to plan around your work, say what comes next. Do not list everything you did since the last update.
- If teammates might wonder whether you need help, say whether anything blocks you. A short “No blockers” answers that.

## When it does not apply

- Your message is not a status update.
- You have no work in progress and no blocker to report.
- The conversation has not settled yet.

It still applies when earlier messages in the thread already say where the work stands or what blocks it. Then a short update that relies on them is a clear update.

## Common mistakes

- Hiding a blocker because it sounds like bad news. The practice looks at how clear your update is, not at whether the work is on track. Say plainly that you’re blocked.
- Padding a short update. Among teammates who already know the work, “Still on it, no blockers” is enough. Add detail when the team needs it to plan or to help.
- Saying you’re stuck with no ask, such as “Kind of stuck on this”. Then a teammate has to reply and ask what you need. Instead, say what you need in the same update.
- Listing everything you did since the last update. That rarely starts a discussion that solves a problem. Instead, name what comes next and what is in the way.

## Sources

- [The Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html#daily-scrum), Schwaber and Sutherland, 2020. The Daily Scrum checks progress toward the goal and helps the team find what blocks its work.
- [Daily stand-up meetings: start breaking the rules](https://doi.org/10.1109/MS.2018.2875988), Stray, Moe and Sjøberg, 2020. In 102 observed stand-ups, a next task or an obstacle usually led to talk about solutions. Past work rarely did.
- [Engineering communication](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/workflow/engineering-comms/#sending-status-updates-to-engineering-fyi), GitLab Handbook. GitLab asks for updates on what was completed and what is blocked or at risk. It also asks you to tag relevant people as needed.
